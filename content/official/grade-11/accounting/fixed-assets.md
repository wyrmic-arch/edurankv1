## What you'll learn

- What fixed (tangible) assets are and how they are recorded at cost.
- How depreciation works and why a business charges it.
- How to calculate depreciation using the straight-line and reducing-balance methods.
- How to record the disposal (sale) of an asset and calculate the profit or loss.
- How to present fixed assets in the Tangible Assets Note of the financial statements.

## Fixed assets and key terms

- **Fixed (tangible) assets** — long-term assets used to earn income, not bought for resale, for example land and buildings, vehicles, equipment and furniture.
- **Cost price** — everything spent to bring the asset into use and into location (purchase price plus delivery, installation, import duties and similar costs, but not repairs).
- **Residual value (scrap value)** — the estimated amount the asset will be worth at the end of its useful life.
- **Useful life** — the number of years the business expects to use the asset.
- **Depreciation** — the systematic write-off (allocation) of the cost of an asset over its useful life.
- **Accumulated depreciation** — the total depreciation charged on an asset since it was bought. It is a **negative (contra) asset** account.
- **Carrying value (book value)** = Cost − Accumulated depreciation.

## Depreciation methods

### Straight-line method

The same amount is written off each year.

Formula: Depreciation = (Cost − Residual value) ÷ Useful life, or simply Cost × rate.

### Reducing-balance (diminishing balance) method

A fixed percentage is applied to the **carrying value**, so the amount written off shrinks each year.

Formula: Depreciation = Carrying value × rate.

If an asset is bought or sold during the year, depreciation is charged only for the months the business owned the asset (pro rata).

## Worked example 1 — Straight-line depreciation and disposal

**Facts (the financial year ends 28/29 February):**

- Equipment bought on 1 March 2021 for R240 000,00.
- Depreciation: 15% per year on cost (straight-line).
- Sold on 31 August 2024 for R100 000,00 cash.

**Step 1 — Annual and monthly depreciation.**

- R240 000,00 × 15% = R36 000,00 per year.
- R36 000,00 ÷ 12 = R3 000,00 per month.

**Step 2 — Depreciation up to the date of sale.**

- 1 March 2021 to 28 February 2024 = 3 full years = 3 × R36 000,00 = R108 000,00.
- 1 March 2024 to 31 August 2024 = 6 months = 6 × R3 000,00 = R18 000,00.
- Total accumulated depreciation = R108 000,00 + R18 000,00 = R126 000,00.

**Step 3 — Carrying value at the date of sale.**

- R240 000,00 − R126 000,00 = R114 000,00.

**Step 4 — Profit or loss on sale.**

- Selling price R100 000,00 − carrying value R114 000,00 = a **loss of R14 000,00**.

**Step 5 — General Journal entries.**

| Details | Debit (R) | Credit (R) |
|---|---|---|
| Asset disposal | 240 000,00 | |
| Equipment | | 240 000,00 |
| Accumulated depreciation on equipment | 126 000,00 | |
| Asset disposal | | 126 000,00 |
| Bank | 100 000,00 | |
| Asset disposal | | 100 000,00 |
| Loss on sale of asset | 14 000,00 | |
| Asset disposal | | 14 000,00 |

**Step 6 — Asset disposal account.**

| Asset disposal | Debit (R) | Credit (R) |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment (cost transferred) | 240 000,00 | |
| Accumulated depreciation (transferred) | | 126 000,00 |
| Bank (sale proceeds) | | 100 000,00 |
| Loss on sale of asset (balancing figure) | | 14 000,00 |
| **Total** | **240 000,00** | **240 000,00** |

The disposal account balances, which proves the entry was recorded correctly.

## Worked example 2 — Reducing-balance depreciation with a part year

**Facts:** A vehicle is bought on 1 April 2022 for R150 000,00. Depreciation is 20% per year on carrying value. The financial year ends 28/29 February.

- Year 1 (1 April 2022 to 28 February 2023 = 11 months): R150 000,00 × 20% × 11/12 = R27 500,00. Carrying value = R122 500,00.
- Year 2 (1 March 2023 to 29 February 2024): R122 500,00 × 20% = R24 500,00. Carrying value = R98 000,00.
- Year 3 (1 March 2024 to 28 February 2025): R98 000,00 × 20% = R19 600,00. Carrying value = R78 400,00.

| Year ended | Carrying value at start (R) | Depreciation (R) | Carrying value at end (R) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 28 February 2023 | 150 000,00 | 27 500,00 | 122 500,00 |
| 29 February 2024 | 122 500,00 | 24 500,00 | 98 000,00 |
| 28 February 2025 | 98 000,00 | 19 600,00 | 78 400,00 |

Notice how the depreciation amount falls each year: that is the defining feature of the reducing-balance method.

## Worked example 3 — Recording depreciation at year end

If a business owns equipment with a cost of R240 000,00 and the depreciation for the year is R36 000,00, the year-end adjustment is:

| Details | Debit (R) | Credit (R) |
|---|---|---|
| Depreciation | 36 000,00 | |
| Accumulated depreciation on equipment | | 36 000,00 |

Depreciation is an expense (debit) and accumulated depreciation increases (credit). The asset account itself is never credited while the asset is still in use.

## Common mistakes

- Charging depreciation on the carrying value when the straight-line method is required, or the other way around.
- Forgetting to charge depreciation for part of a year when an asset was bought or sold during the year.
- Crediting the asset account directly for depreciation instead of using the accumulated depreciation account.
- Using the carrying value instead of the accumulated depreciation when transferring amounts on disposal.
- Getting the profit or loss on disposal the wrong way round: it is selling price minus carrying value.
- Including repairs and maintenance in the cost price of an asset (only costs to bring it into use are capitalised).
- Forgetting that land is normally not depreciated, while buildings are.

## Quick summary / cheat sheet

- Carrying value = Cost − Accumulated depreciation.
- Straight-line: Cost × rate (the same amount every year).
- Reducing balance: Carrying value × rate (the amount shrinks every year).
- Part year: multiply the yearly figure by months ÷ 12.
- Disposal: transfer the cost, transfer the accumulated depreciation, record the sale, then balance the disposal account to find the profit or loss.
- Profit = selling price greater than carrying value. Loss = selling price less than carrying value.
- Depreciation entry: Debit Depreciation, Credit Accumulated depreciation.
- The asset account is credited (removed) only when the asset is sold or scrapped.

## Practice questions

1. A machine costs R180 000,00 and is depreciated at 20% per year on cost. What is the depreciation for one year?
2. Using the machine in question 1 (straight-line), what is the carrying value after 3 years?
3. A vehicle with a carrying value of R90 000,00 is sold for R82 000,00. Is there a profit or a loss, and how much?
4. An asset bought on 1 July for R120 000,00 is depreciated at 15% per year on cost. The year ends 28 February. Calculate the depreciation for the first year.
5. A delivery van has a carrying value of R64 000,00. Depreciation is 25% per year on the reducing-balance method. What is the depreciation for the next year?
6. Name the two accounts used when recording depreciation for a year.
7. Why does a business charge depreciation at all?

### Answers

1. R180 000,00 × 20% = **R36 000,00**.
2. R180 000,00 − (3 × R36 000,00) = R180 000,00 − R108 000,00 = **R72 000,00**.
3. R82 000,00 − R90 000,00 = a **loss of R8 000,00**.
4. 8 months (July to February): R120 000,00 × 15% × 8/12 = **R12 000,00**.
5. R64 000,00 × 25% = **R16 000,00**.
6. Depreciation (an expense) and Accumulated depreciation (a contra-asset).
7. Because a fixed asset helps to earn income over several years, so its cost is spread (allocated) over its useful life to match the income it helps produce.
