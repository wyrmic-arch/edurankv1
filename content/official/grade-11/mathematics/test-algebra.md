## What you'll learn

- How to solve linear, quadratic and simultaneous equations.
- How to work with inequalities and represent solutions on a number line.
- The nature of roots using the discriminant.

## Key ideas

An **equation** states that two expressions are equal. An **inequality** states
that one is greater or less than the other. When you multiply or divide an
inequality by a negative number, the inequality sign flips.

### The quadratic formula

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

The expression under the root, $b^2 - 4ac$, is the **discriminant** and tells you
how many real roots there are.

| Discriminant | Nature of roots |
| --- | --- |
| $b^2 - 4ac > 0$ | Two distinct real roots |
| $b^2 - 4ac = 0$ | One repeated real root |
| $b^2 - 4ac < 0$ | No real roots |

## Worked example

Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$.

Factorise: $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$, so $x = 2$ or $x = 3$.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to flip the inequality sign when multiplying by a negative.
- Cancelling terms incorrectly across an equation.

## Quick summary

- Quadratic formula solves any quadratic.
- The discriminant tells you the nature of the roots.
- Always check inequality sign direction.

## Practice

1. Solve $x^2 + 2x - 8 = 0$.
2. Solve $2x + 3 > 11$.

**Answers:** 1) $x = 2$ or $x = -4$. 2) $x > 4$.
